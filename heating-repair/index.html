Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

Heating repair carries the highest stakes in home comfort work because combustion is involved. A furnace that lights late booms gas vapor inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal; a heat exchanger that cracks can pass combustion gases into the supply air; a flue that has stopped drafting sends carbon monoxide into the house instead of out of it, and none of these announce themselves in ways an untrained eye can catch. The diagnostic terrain is genuinely tricky too: a furnace that short-cycles might have a failing limit switch, or it might be protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a flame sensor without asking why it fouled leaves the next lockout already scheduled.

That is why Westminster HVAC does not guess. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate so the blower and the fire are properly matched. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve before blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because your family's warmth and safety depend on it.

How to Prevent Heating Repair Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Make sure every level has a working carbon monoxide detector, and test it monthly
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
