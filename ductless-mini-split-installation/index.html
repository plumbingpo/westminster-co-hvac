Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Westminster home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. There is no ductwork to leak or lose energy, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it works as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Good ductless comfort starts with a load calculation, not a catalog. We size each zone to what that room actually needs, since an oversized head short-cycles, leaves the air clammy, and never quite holds temperature. With multiple zones, the engineering gets stricter: capacity has to be split sensibly, and line set length and elevation change must stay inside the manufacturer's tables or the system quietly loses performance. Style matters too, because a wall head, a ceiling cassette, and a low floor unit each change both airflow and the look of the room. Our experienced technicians review every room in person, agree on placements that work physically and visually, and plan line set routes that stay hidden where possible and tidy where they show.

On installation day, the small steps that customers never see are the ones we never rush. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a leaking flare joint is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We make sure condensate drains with proper fall or a dependable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. You get a walkthrough of the remote that covers the modes worth using. Call (970) 602-4926 for a Westminster assessment and a clear quote, whether you need one zone or the whole house.

The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion creates a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors handle badly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall leaves a water stain the first humid week.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head short-cycles, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Westminster HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and commissioning each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a place to improvise. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of one that mysteriously underperforms.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Schedule professional cleaning of the indoor coil and blower wheel before buildup turns into growth
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, leaves, and grass clippings in every season
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
